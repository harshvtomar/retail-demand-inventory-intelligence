# Session 2: advanced analytics and demand-feature methodology

**Scope:** Real user-authorized Walmart M5 data, but only a **deterministic nonrandom 210 product-store-series sample** of the 30,490 original source series; 401,730 product-store-day observations, 2011-01-29 through 2016-04-24. Store- or category-wide inferences to the M5 population are **not** justified. The 58,327,370 source observations were audited in Session 1, not loaded into the current warehouse.

## Advanced SQL
`sql/advanced_queries.sql` provides **14 additional, executable analytical queries** beyond Session 1's 12 queries. Execute `python -m src.advanced_sql` to generate *private* detailed SQL outputs under `data/processed/session2_sql`. Then run `python -m scripts.reconcile_session2`. The reference process rebuilds the same nonrandom series sample directly from source CSVs (not the SQLite warehouse) and checks row keys and numeric fields from all 14 reports within absolute tolerance 1e-6. SQL queries 01/14 include rolling windows; query 14 **excludes the target day's units**, avoiding direct leakage. Week numbers in query 13 use calendar `%Y-%W` and are intended for descriptive exploration, not time-series training folds.

## ABC: observed sales units, not inventory value
Series are ranked by descending observed unit totals, with deterministic item/store tie breaking. The item crossing the 80% cumulative-unit threshold is assigned to A; the item crossing 95% goes to B; remaining positive unit-volume series go to C. Equivalently, the **preceding** cumulative percentage determines tier membership. Series with zero total units go to U. Class thresholds are configurable, not universal policy. The ABC classes are computed over all sample dates **for retrospective descriptive analysis only**. Recomputing classes using training-history-only data is mandatory before any predictive use; otherwise validation and held-out performance will be contaminated by future demand.

## XYZ: coefficient of variation of weekly observed units
For each series aggregate sales on complete Monday–Sunday calendar weeks (`observed_days=7`). Exclude incomplete boundary weeks rather than comparing partial against full weeks. Population CV = population standard deviation of weekly observed unit totals / mean weekly observed unit totals. X: CV ≤ 0.5; Y: 0.5 < CV ≤ 1; Z: CV > 1; U: undefined or zero mean. Thresholds are configurable heuristics. Values from daily sales often differ from weekly CV. No safety stock, reorder level or service-level guarantee is inferred.

## Intermittent-demand diagnostics
Define `ADI = observed days / positive-sales days` (undefined if no positives). Define `CV² = population variance(positive sales sizes) / mean(positive sizes)^2` if at least two positive observations exist, else undefined. Diagnostic cutoffs: ADI 1.32 and CV² 0.49. ADI ≤ 1.32 and CV² ≤ 0.49 -> Smooth; high ADI + low CV² -> Intermittent; low ADI + high CV² -> Erratic; both high -> Lumpy; insufficient observations -> U. Classifications reflect **observed** sales, not known customer demand: zeros may be caused by demand, stockouts, assortment, recording, or calendar effects.

## Predictive feature availability and leakage
`src.session2.daily_features` defines **one-day-ahead** labels at target date t. Lags 1/7/14/28/56 are from t-k; prior moving means, standard deviations, and zero shares use only dates t-1 back to t-window; a missing history window is NaN rather than improperly including partial data. Day-of-week, month and weekend are date-derived, known in advance. Series keys group all rolling computations; no contamination between stores or items.

`src.session2.rolling_origins_28` is a *different* supervised design: after observing units through origin date t, the target is the sum of units for t+1 through t+28 inclusive. Origin-day units are available at this end-of-day forecasting origin. The future 28-day sum is a **label** stored only for model development/evaluation, not a feature. Forecast origins are anchored backwards in 28-day steps from the final possible origin, with 56 days of prior history. This design assumes consecutive daily observations; otherwise generation fails loudly. No future realized price is used as a feature. Do not combine the one-day-ahead `target_day_units` with 28-day horizon features for scoring future periods.

## Suggested Session 3 evaluation policy
Historical span ends **2016-04-24** in the existing sample. Freeze the *last 28 days* (2016-03-28 through 2016-04-24) as the final untouched test window, with origin **2016-03-27**. Use at least three earlier 28-day windows (for example 2015-12-07 onward, subject to origin availability) as rolling validation folds, leaving a reasonable multi-year historical training period. At each origin: fit preprocessing/scalers/segmentations exclusively on history available at that origin; form predictions for the full next 28 days without leaking actual intermediate-day units; evaluate total 28-day series demand and optionally more granular outputs if the forecast model is properly recursive/known-in-advance. Compare zero-demand, seasonal-naïve (last four weeks) and a rolling-mean baseline before more complex models. Report WAPE with zero-denominator flag, MAE, RMSE, bias and MASE with a valid nonzero seasonal scaling denominator; report performance both across all series and segmented after **fold-safe** classifications. Do not tune hyperparameters on the held-out period.

## Business interpretation limits
This is a portfolio case study using genuine historical competition data. It is *not* evidence of real inventories, purchasing decisions, stockout occurrences, profitability, or causal effects of retailer promotions. Seasonal and event comparisons are descriptive and potentially confounded. Unit ABC and weekly CV are screening tools, not optimized procurement rules.
